**FIREARCHY** was my team's entry to the **NASA × Blackstone Innovation Challenge**: a framework for finding archaeological sites by routing NASA pattern-recognition tech through a remote-sensing + machine-learning pipeline.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/firearchy_presentation.jpg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}

## The idea

- Researched applications of the **Groff Algorithm** for pattern recognition in satellite imagery.
- Built a framework for identifying potential archaeological sites via ML + remote sensing — the same instinct that later matured into the [Llanos de Moxos work](/projects/10_project/).
- Investigated NASA patents around sustainability and climate analysis to understand what's dual-use.
- Explored triangulation capabilities for location-based pattern recognition.

## Hardware side (yes, there was one)

The team prototyped physical components — chargers, filters, and housings — in **SolidWorks** and 3D-printed them. The repo still has the CAD files (`.SLDPRT`, `.STL`, assembly animations) and the pitch decks (`Fire-Archy.pptx`, `Fire-Archy 2.pptx`).

## Why it mattered

FIREARCHY was my first *built* argument for the thesis I now organize everything around: cutting-edge sensing + AI, used carefully, lets you discover and *protect* cultural heritage without ever disturbing it. The archaeological-remote-sensing lineage that runs through my later Florida Museum and FURC work starts here.
