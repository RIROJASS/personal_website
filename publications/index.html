  1. ShortKit-ML
    Shortkit-ML: A Unified Multi-Perspective Framework for Detecting Shortcut Learning in Medical Imaging Embeddings
    Sebastian Cajas, Aldo Marzullo, Sahil Kapadia, and 8 more authors
    IEEE Access (in review); preprint on medRxiv. Status: in review at IEEE Access; preprint on medRxiv. , 2026
    Abs Bib HTML PDF Code

    A unified framework to detect, benchmark, and mitigate shortcut learning in ML models — across medical imaging and beyond. Combines 20+ detection methods with 6 mitigation techniques under one API, with multi-attribute sensitive-attribute support. Benchmarked on CheXpert, MIMIC-CXR, and CelebA; released on PyPI under MIT. Raúl contributed the unifying ontology bridging anthropological and ML definitions of "shortcut."

  2. Credentials
    Credential Farming in the Age of Generative AI: Relocating Trust from Outputs to Communities
    Gift Osei Agyemang, Rawad AL-Louzi, Melat Mezgebu Bawoke, and 13 more authors
    Manuscript submitted. Status: submitted. , 2026
    Abs Bib

    Credentials began as proxies for competence, but generative AI has weakened the inference from research outputs to the capacities those outputs were meant to signal — producing a gaming dynamic in which effort shifts toward optimizing the signal rather than the learning it was supposed to represent. The paper argues academic trust must relocate from manufactured outputs to formation inside research communities. Authors listed alphabetically by surname.

  3. SAKSHI
    Community Auditing of Health Artificial Intelligence in Low- and Middle-Income Countries: The SAKSHI Investigative Protocol
    Alexa Q. Xiang, Alexander Cole Bank, Josephine Nanyonjo, and 14 more authors
    Manuscript submitted. Status: submitted. , 2026
    Abs Bib

    SAKSHI (Structured Accountability through Knowledge, Scrutiny, and Health Integrity) is a community-operated investigative platform supplying independent scrutiny of health-AI deployment in low- and middle-income countries — settings where regulatory frameworks have not kept pace with autonomous diagnostic and prescribing tools, and where tort systems offer little practical recourse for patients who are harmed.

  4. Hallucinations
    Hallucinations Are the Least of Our Concerns: Situating Knowledge for Responsible AI in Health
    Alexa Q. Xiang, Andres Rico, Biruk Zenebe Bekele, and 10 more authors
    Manuscript in preparation. Status: in preparation. , 2026
    Abs Bib

    Argues that situated knowledge — not reference-checking — is the prerequisite for responsible large-language-model use in health. Reframes "hallucinations" as a secondary problem compared to the deeper gap between model outputs and the local knowledge contexts where health decisions actually land.

  5. Oracle
    The Oracle in Your Pocket: How Artificial Intelligence May Amplify the Dunning-Kruger Effect and Why Epistemic Humility Matters
    Rogers Mwavu, Cecilia Cocucci, Sofiya Mikolutskaya, and 10 more authors
    Manuscript in preparation. Status: in preparation; submission not yet confirmed. , 2026
    Abs Bib

    Examines how repeated use of large language models may alter users’ perceptions of their own expertise. Draws on cognitive science (Dunning-Kruger, metacognition, cognitive offloading, cognitive surrender) to argue for epistemic humility and sharper AI literacy, with emphasis on health misinformation, Indigenous knowledge systems, and science communication.

  6. AGORA
    AGORA Peer Review: A Measurement-First Architecture for Auditable Scientific Agent Systems
    AGORA Working Group, MIT Critical Data
    In development. Status: in development. , 2026
    Abs Bib

    An experimental research programme for studying AI-mediated scientific peer review as an auditable multi-agent system. Separates measurement from judgement, single-agent behaviour from multi-agent interaction, and observed evidence from future protocol design. On the team and contributing to early-stage work.
