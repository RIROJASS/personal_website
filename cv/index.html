Publications

  • 2026
    Shortkit-ML: A Unified Multi-Perspective Framework for Detecting Shortcut Learning in Medical Imaging Embeddings
    IEEE Access (in review); preprint on medRxiv

    Unified framework to detect, benchmark, and mitigate shortcut learning in ML models — across medical imaging and beyond. 20+ detection methods plus 6 mitigation techniques under one API, benchmarked on CheXpert, MIMIC-CXR, and CelebA. Released on PyPI under MIT License. My contribution was the unifying ontology bridging anthropological and ML definitions of “shortcut.”

  • 2026
    Credential Farming in the Age of Generative AI: Relocating Trust from Outputs to Communities
    Manuscript submitted

    Generative AI has weakened the inference from research outputs to the competencies they were meant to signal — producing a gaming dynamic where effort shifts toward optimizing the credential rather than the learning behind it. The paper argues academic trust must relocate from manufactured outputs to formation inside research communities. Authors listed alphabetically by surname.

  • 2026
    Community Auditing of Health AI in Low- and Middle-Income Countries: The SAKSHI Investigative Protocol
    Manuscript submitted

    SAKSHI (Structured Accountability through Knowledge, Scrutiny, and Health Integrity) is a community-operated investigative platform supplying independent scrutiny of health-AI deployment in LMICs — settings where regulatory frameworks have not kept pace with autonomous diagnostic and prescribing tools, and where tort systems offer little recourse for patients who are harmed.

  • 2026
    Hallucinations Are the Least of Our Concerns: Situating Knowledge for Responsible AI in Health
    Manuscript in preparation

    Argues that situated knowledge — not reference-checking — is the prerequisite for responsible LLM use in health. Reframes “hallucinations” as a secondary concern compared to the deeper gap between model outputs and the local knowledge contexts where health decisions actually land.

  • 2026
    The Oracle in Your Pocket: How AI May Amplify the Dunning-Kruger Effect and Why Epistemic Humility Matters
    Manuscript in preparation (submission not yet confirmed)

    Examines how repeated use of LLMs may alter users’ perceptions of their own expertise. Draws on cognitive science (Dunning-Kruger, metacognition, cognitive offloading, cognitive surrender) to argue for epistemic humility and sharper AI literacy — with emphasis on health misinformation, Indigenous knowledge systems, and science communication.

  • 2026
    AGORA Peer Review: A Measurement-First Architecture for Auditable Scientific Agent Systems
    In development

    An experimental research programme for studying AI-mediated scientific peer review as an auditable multi-agent system. On the team; contributing to early-stage work.
