Spent a day at **Innovation Summit 2024**. Three speakers mattered, and one VC panel put the money side into context.

## Speakers

- **Brian Mawdsley** *(Deputy CIO @ UFICO)* — on finding meaning in work and investment analysis. Pushed hard on identifying the *next* thing rather than reasoning retroactively about what won. Quote: *"Focus on the compass, not the clock."*
- **Jackson Streeter** *(Director @ UF Ventures)* — the entrepreneur counterweight to Brian's investor lens. Big on clean energy, business ecosystems, and the point that you should obsess over individual customers before you obsess over market trends.
- **Jack Kendall** *(CTO of [Rain AI](https://rain.ai/))* — on **neuromorphic computing** and the future of AI compute. Shared the arc of developing patent-worthy work and underlined how often breakthroughs sit on multidisciplinary ground. The most directly relevant speaker to my BCI direction.

## VC panel

- **Ryan Pruitt** *(Managing Director @ Ocean Azul Partners)*
- **Olivia Gaudree** *(Managing Director @ Fuel VC)*
- **Chris Langford** *(Managing Partner @ Home Tech Ventures)*

The panel surfaced what VCs actually look for in founders — not just the pitch, but *passion, expertise, and character* as a package deal.

## What I'm taking back

The Rain AI conversation was the one I keep coming back to. Neuromorphic hardware and real-time BCI/EEG are converging on the same bottleneck from opposite ends — one builds a chip that thinks like a brain, the other reads from an actual brain. There's a company-shaped hole between them.
