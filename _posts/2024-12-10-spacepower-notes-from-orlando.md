I went to **Spacepower 2024** in Orlando as one of five winners of the **UF Space Initiative 2024 Innovation Challenge**. The **Space Force Association** previewed our team in its official magazine, calling us *"a dynamic group who embodied the power of cross-disciplinary collaboration."* That line — and the bright orange **WINNER** badge on my lanyard — made this one a real personal milestone.

## Why it mattered

- **First serious dive into the space sector.** It was the first room where my anthropology habit and my AI toolkit were obviously on the same side.
- **Startup-first atmosphere.** Panels on venture funding, SpaceWERX accelerators, and small-sat constellations showed how fast ideas move from lab to launchpad.
- **Hands-on demos.** Tried an AI-enabled space-debris tracker, tested a digital-twin trainer for Guardians, and saw live inference on orbit-qualified edge GPUs.

## Conference highlights

- **Maj Gen Timothy Sejba** *(Commander, STARCOM)* — keynote on building a "purpose-built" Space Force ready for Great-Power competition.
- **Startup showcase** — 30+ early-stage companies pitching propulsion, on-orbit servicing, and AI autonomy. Many funded through the SpaceWERX Tactical Funding Increase program.
- **Gen-AI Challenge Finals** — teams used Guardian fitness telemetry to generate personalized training plans. This is exactly the kind of multimodal pipeline work I enjoy — pulling signal from sensors you'd normally ignore.
- One quote stuck with me: *"Innovation is our orbital maneuver; agility is our delta-V."* — SFA President William Woolf.

## Take-aways

- Identified potential mentors in **model-based systems engineering (MBSE)** and **satellite-AI inference** for follow-up.
- Sketched a capstone idea: an AI-powered, culture-aware knowledge base for long-duration missions — merging anthropology and CS.
- Brought home concrete design cues for my own projects (edge ML for in-remote-sensing decision-making).
